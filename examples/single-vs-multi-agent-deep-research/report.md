## Definitions and framing

For this report:

- **Single-agent** = one model, one context window, one ReAct/planner loop. Tool calls and "reasoning" steps are interleaved in the same trace. OpenAI Deep Research, Gemini Deep Research, and Perplexity Deep Research all fit this shape: a single LLM trained or prompted to plan, browse, read, and iterate inside one context [9][12][14][24].
- **Multi-agent** = an orchestrator (or planner) that spawns ≥2 LLM workers with **separate context windows**, structured task contracts, and an explicit synthesis step. Anthropic's Research feature [1] and STORM [17] are canonical instances.
- **Hybrid** = single planner + parallel tool calls but no full subagents; researcher+writer two-agent splits; critic/verifier loops. GPT-Researcher exposes both modes as configuration choices [20][21][23].

The "single vs multi" line is fuzzy. Vendors prefer "agentic" precisely because it elides this distinction. LiveResearchBench draws the line based on whether independent context windows exist [24].

## Evidence by axis

### Quality

The cleanest published quality lift for multi-agent comes from Anthropic: **+90.2% over single-agent Claude Opus 4** on their internal research evaluation, with the largest gains on breadth-first queries like "identify all board members of the IT companies in the S&P 500" [1][2][3]. Anthropic attributes this primarily to **scaled token usage across parallel context windows**, reporting that on BrowseComp, three factors explain 95% of performance variance with **token usage alone explaining ~80%**, plus tool-call count and model choice [1]. ◐

Single-agent products lead on the most-cited public benchmarks. **OpenAI Deep Research** (a single tool-use-tuned o3 model) reports 26.6% on Humanity's Last Exam with browsing + Python and a state-of-the-art GAIA score of **67.36% pass@1 / 72.57% cons@64** (Level 1 78.66, Level 2 73.21, Level 3 58.03) [7][12]. **Gemini Deep Research** has climbed sharply — the Dec 11, 2025 release on Gemini 3 Pro reports **46.4% HLE, 66.1% on Google's DeepSearchQA, and 59.2% on BrowseComp** [8]; an earlier Gemini 2.5 Pro version reported 26.9–32.4% HLE [13][14]. **Perplexity Deep Research** at launch reported **21.1% HLE and 93.9% SimpleQA**, completing most queries in 2–4 minutes [9][10].

Open-source baselines: STORM is multi-agent (perspective-guided "Wikipedia writer" agents simulate conversations with a topic expert; a curator/writer pipeline drafts the article) and reports 92.73% heading soft-recall and 45.91% heading entity recall on FreshWiki with GPT-4 [17][18][19]. On the live DeepScholar-Bench research-synthesis benchmark, STORM with Llama-4-Scout scores 0.119 organization / 0.183 nugget coverage / 0.586 claim coverage — substantially below OpenAI Deep Research on the same metrics (0.857 / 0.392 / 0.138, with the inverse claim-coverage relationship reflecting differing citation styles) [16]. GPT-Researcher claims top rank on CMU's DeepResearchGym, but this is self-reported on its homepage and not independently verified [23]. ?

### Cost

Anthropic's **15× chat-token figure for multi-agent and 4× for single-agent tool use** [1][3] is the most-cited public number and comes with a hard guidance line: multi-agent "only pencils out for high-value tasks involving heavy parallelization, information that exceeds a single 200K context window, or interfacing with many complex tools" [1]. ✓

Per-query economics for the products are unevenly disclosed. Perplexity Sonar Deep Research has transparent token pricing and tier examples ($0.41 low / $1.19 medium / $1.32 high per query in Perplexity's own examples) [9]. OpenAI Deep Research takes 5–30 minutes per query and is bundled in subscriptions (25 queries/mo on Plus, 250 on Pro) [12]. Anthropic does not publish per-query cost for the consumer Research feature [1].

### Latency

Parallelism is the only lever Anthropic explicitly identifies. Their early sequential implementation was "painfully slow"; two changes — the lead spawning **3–5 subagents concurrently** and each subagent issuing **3+ tool calls in parallel** — cut research time **by up to 90% on complex queries** [1][2]. The residual bottleneck they call out: lead agents currently run subagents synchronously, preventing mid-flight steering and subagent-to-subagent coordination; asynchronous execution is on the roadmap [1]. ◐

For interactive sub-30-second use, single-agent loops dominate. Perplexity's 2–4 min target is already too slow for chat-style use [9][10]; OpenAI Deep Research's 5–30 min range is firmly batch [12].

### Context-window pressure

The 2024–2026 long-context literature converges: long single-agent traces have measurable, architectural pathologies.

**Lost-in-the-middle** (Liu et al., TACL 2024) shows a U-shaped accuracy curve where GPT-3.5-Turbo's multi-document QA accuracy drops by **>20 points — falling below closed-book performance** — when the gold document sits in the middle of 20–30 retrieved documents. The effect persists in GPT-4 and in extended-context variants like GPT-3.5-Turbo (16K) and Claude-1.3-100K; longer context windows do not by themselves fix the problem [25][27][29]. Hsieh et al. show this is an **intrinsic positional-attention bias** that calibration can recover up to **15 percentage points** of [26]. ✓

**LongPiBench** (Tian et al., 2024 rev. 2025) extends the analysis to multi-needle queries and finds modern frontier models are largely robust to absolute position but still show **20–30% performance fluctuations from relative spacing alone**, even at fixed 32K input [28]. ◐

**Context Rot** (Chroma, July 2025) is the strongest evidence for a structural problem. Across 18 frontier models — Claude Opus 4, Sonnet 4, Sonnet 3.7/3.5; GPT-4.1, GPT-4o, GPT-4 Turbo, o3; Gemini 2.5 Pro/Flash; Qwen3-235B/32B/8B — **every single model degrades as input length grows, even on tasks held complexity-constant** (non-lexical needle retrieval, distractor-laden NIAH, LongMemEval, even trivial repeated-word reproduction) [30]. ✓

These results explain why production multi-agent designs persist despite the token cost: distributing work across **separate** 200K context windows is the architectural antidote to context rot. Anthropic explicitly notes their LeadResearcher persists its plan to an external Memory store because conversations regularly exceed the 200K-token limit and would otherwise be truncated [1][2].

### Reliability and failure modes

Multi-agent failures are concretely documented in Anthropic's post: spawning 50 subagents for trivial queries; endless searches for nonexistent sources; subagents distracting each other with excessive status updates; **duplicated work from vague delegation** (the canonical "research the semiconductor shortage" example, where one subagent looked at the 2021 auto-chip crisis while two others redundantly searched 2025 supply chains); over-long search queries returning no results; and a preference for **SEO-optimized content farms over authoritative academic/primary sources** that only human evaluators caught [1][2][3]. ✓

Anthropic's mitigations are equally specific: scaling rules embedded in prompts (1 agent / 3–10 calls for fact-finding; 2–4 subagents / 10–15 calls for comparisons; 10+ for complex research); a **tool-testing agent that auto-rewrites flawed MCP tool descriptions**, yielding a 40% reduction in completion time for downstream agents; checkpointing, retry logic, resumable execution; and full production tracing because of non-deterministic behavior [1][4]. ✓

Single-agent failures cluster around the long-context pathologies above plus compound errors over long autonomous traces.

### Citation faithfulness

This is the axis where vendor disclosure is thinnest. Anthropic uses a separate **CitationAgent** that runs after research concludes to attribute claims to specific source spans [1][2]. STORM has a writer/curator pipeline but does not report quantitative citation faithfulness on standard benchmarks [17][19]. The DeepScholar-Bench citation-precision number for OpenAI Deep Research is 0.399 — non-trivial and below STORM's 0.238 only on a different scoring axis [16]. FRAMES, the Google DeepMind 824-question multi-hop benchmark, evaluates factuality + retrieval + reasoning together and reports a multi-step retrieval baseline on Gemini-Pro-1.5 of 0.66 [15]; FRAMES leaderboard data for the named consumer Deep Research products is largely absent — only retrieval-engine-level numbers from Perplexity's `search_evals` harness are public, and those are tied to specific search-engine + LLM combinations rather than the consumer "Perplexity Deep Research" product [11].

### Observability and engineering complexity

Anthropic identifies multi-agent observability as a primary engineering challenge: "minor changes to a lead agent unpredictably alter subagent behavior," runs are non-deterministic, and the team had to instrument **full production tracing of agent decisions** plus **rainbow deployments** to avoid breaking long-running agents during code rollouts [1][4]. They also publish an **LLM-as-judge rubric** covering factual accuracy, citation accuracy, completeness, source quality, and tool efficiency, supplemented by human evaluation, with a recommended starting set of ~20 representative queries [1]. ✓

Single-agent traces are linear and standard tracing/eval tools work without adaptation. The trade-off is exactly what observability buys: multi-agent systems need it to debug at all.

### Security / prompt-injection blast radius

Cited sources do not provide quantitative blast-radius numbers for multi-agent vs single-agent. Architecturally the surface is larger in multi-agent: each subagent has its own tool access and typically trusts upstream output, so a single poisoned source (web page, shared memory entry) can hijack downstream subagents that have action-taking tools. LiveResearchBench's Knowledge Gaps section flags safety and tool-trust evaluation as unaddressed [24]. ?
