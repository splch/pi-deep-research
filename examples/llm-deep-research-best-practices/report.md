## Canonical Multi-Agent Architecture and Where the Leading Systems Actually Differ

Anthropic's June 2025 engineering post is the only vendor primary source that describes the full orchestrator/worker/citation-auditor pattern verbatim: a `LeadResearcher` (Opus 4) classifies the query and persists the plan to Memory to survive 200K-token truncation; it spawns 3–5 Sonnet 4 subagents in parallel — each with its own 200K context window — that themselves call 3+ tools in parallel; results funnel through a dedicated `CitationAgent` that maps claims to source spans before the report is returned [fact-check: ✅] ✓ [1][2][13]. Anthropic embeds explicit effort-scaling rules in the lead's system prompt (1 agent / 3–10 calls for fact-finding; 2–4 subagents / 10–15 calls each for comparisons; 10+ for complex research) precisely because early agents "spawned 50 subagents for simple queries" and "scoured the web endlessly for nonexistent sources" ◐ [1]. Parallelism is the load-bearing claim — Anthropic reports up to 90% wall-clock reduction and a 90.2% multi-agent uplift over single-agent Opus 4 on an internal eval [fact-check: ✅] — but the same post concedes ~15× the tokens of a chat session, sync-only execution, and unsuitability for tasks with cross-agent dependencies (most coding) ◐ [1][2].

OpenAI's Deep Research system card describes a different architecture: an early version of o3 trained end-to-end via RL on browsing trajectories (search/click/scroll/file-interpret) plus a sandboxed Python tool with no internet access, with a separate o3-mini model only used to summarize chains of thought for display ✓ [3][15]. There is no published planner/worker/writer split. The principal prompt-injection defense is system-level: the agent is forbidden from "navigat[ing] to or construct[ing] arbitrary URLs," specifically to prevent API-key exfiltration via URL parameters — an architectural lethal-trifecta cut, not a delegation pattern ✓ [3]. Post-mitigation attack-success rates fall to 0.00–2.63% across categories ✓ [3].

Google's Gemini Deep Research is a tiered single-agent loop. The April 2026 "Deep Research Max" announcement describes two SKUs that swap test-time compute rather than spawn workers: `deep-research-preview-04-2026` budgets ~80 searches and ~250K input / ~60K output tokens at ~$1–3/task; `deep-research-max-preview-04-2026` extends to ~160 searches and ~900K input / ~80K output tokens at ~$3–7/task, with 50–70% of input tokens cached [fact-check: ✅] ✓ [4][5]. Default tools are Google Search, URL Context, and Code Execution; MCP servers and File Search are optional; `collaborative_planning=true` exposes the plan for human review; `background=true` is required and the maximum research time is 60 minutes ✓ [4][5].

Perplexity is the least documented. Its Sonar Deep Research is described as "multi-step retrieval, synthesis, and reasoning" on Llama 3.3 70B, but no planner/worker/writer/auditor decomposition is published ◐ (worker noted Perplexity's blog could not be directly fetched in the run).

Sources differ on whether multi-agent is the right default. **Cognition's "Don't Build Multi-Agents" (June 2025)** argues parallel sub-agents systematically violate two principles — share full context, and avoid parallel actions encoding conflicting implicit decisions — and recommends single-threaded agents with optional context compression ✓ [30]. LangChain's open_deep_research, the closest open analogue to Anthropic's pattern, biases the supervisor toward a single agent in its lead prompt and writes one-shot after research is complete because earlier section-parallel writers produced disjoint reports ◐ (worker_findings).

## Citation and Grounding Standards

Three primitives anchor the field. **FActScore** (Min et al., EMNLP 2023) defines factual precision as the share of atomic facts in a long-form output that a knowledge source supports; automated FActScore tracks human within ~2% error ✓ [6][7]. **SAFE** (Wei et al., NeurIPS 2024) generalises this to the open web — atomise, self-contain, Google-search, judge — with ~72% crowd agreement on ~16k facts, 76% wins on re-adjudication, and ~20× lower cost than humans (~$0.19 vs ~$4 per response); SAFE also introduces **F1@K**, where K is a length proxy that gives recall a footing in long-form ✓ [8][9]. **AIS** (Rashkin et al., CL 49(4) 2023) is the formal "Attributable to Identified Sources" definition — a generic listener concludes "According to P, s" — that underpins all attribution-F1 metrics ✓ [10][11]. **ALCE** (Gao et al., EMNLP 2023) is the canonical inline-`[N]` benchmark, scoring fluency + correctness + citation precision/recall/F1 and showing top LLMs lacked complete citation support on ~50% of ELI5 sentences — citation presence ≠ citation validity ✓ [12].

Production systems wire these together as a separate post-synthesis pass. Anthropic's `CitationAgent` ingests documents + the draft report and identifies source locations for claims before return ✓ [1][13]; its single-judge LLM-as-judge rubric explicitly separates factual accuracy ("do claims match sources?") from citation accuracy ("do cited sources match claims?"), with completeness, source quality, and tool efficiency as additional axes ◐ [1]. **Google DeepMind's FACTS Grounding** (1,719 examples) judges in two phases — eligibility (does the response actually address the request?) and grounding (every claim supported, no hallucinations) — disqualifying ineligible responses to prevent hedging gaming the metric ✓ [14].

The citation auditor's other job is **URL hygiene**. Rao, Wong & Callison-Burch (arXiv 2604.03173, 2026) measured 53,090 URLs from DRBench and 168,021 from ExpertQA across 10 commercial models: 3–13% hallucinated and 5–18% non-resolving overall, with deep-research agents fabricating more than single-shot LLMs (10.7% vs 4.8%, p<10⁻⁵¹) [fact-check: ✅] ◐ [16]. Their open-source `urlhealth` tool — HTTP HEAD with GET fallback on 405/403/501, Wayback lookup on 404 to split "hallucinated" from "stale," and a separate UNKNOWN bucket for paywalls — when used in an agentic self-correction loop reduced non-resolving rates 6–79× to under 1% (GPT-5.1 16.0%→0.6%; Gemini 6.1%→0.1%; Claude 4.9%→0.8%), but only when the model's tool-use is competent [fact-check: ✅] ◐ [16]. Domain failure rates vary ~2× across fields and up to 4.3× per-model — motivating domain-aware verification bars ◐ [16]. OpenAI's system card reports PersonQA hallucination rate 0.13 (vs 0.30 for GPT-4o, 0.20 for o1), attributing the gap to live-search grounding ✓ [15].

## Search/Fetch Tool Design

**Anthropic's `web_fetch`** [20] exposes the practitioner-actionable surface: `max_uses`, mutually exclusive `allowed_domains`/`blocked_domains` (subdomains auto-included), `max_content_tokens`, and a citations block with `cited_text` + `start_char_index`/`end_char_index` + `retrieved_at` for provenance [fact-check: ✅] ✓ [20]. Anthropic publishes typical token costs — ~2,500 tokens for 10 kB, ~25,000 for 100 kB, ~125,000 for a 500 kB PDF — making `max_content_tokens` the practical lever against a single hostile or oversized page swallowing context ✓ [20]. **Tavily** [21] caps `include_domains` (≤300) / `exclude_domains` (≤150), exposes `time_range` + `start_date`/`end_date` + `country`, defaults `max_results=5` (1–20, with explicit warning that higher values "may return lower-quality results"), and recommends a two-step Search→Extract pattern over `include_raw_content=true` ✓ [21]. **Exa** [22] supports `includeDomains`/`excludeDomains` (≤1,200), date filters, a `category` enum, and — most importantly for context-budget engineering — `highlights: true` and `contents.text.maxCharacters` for token-efficient excerpts that Exa documents as ~10× cheaper than full text ✓ [22]. None of these vendors expose a native per-domain diversity cap; "cite at most N URLs from any one host" must be enforced in orchestrator code ? (worker_findings).

## Effort/Cost Controls

The token-cost envelope is the dominant constraint. Anthropic's stepped subagent budget (1 / 2-3 / 3-5 / 5-10, hard cap 20) and per-worker tool-call limits (under 5 / 5 / 10 / 15 by complexity, terminating at 20 calls or 100 sources) are encoded directly in the verbatim cookbook prompts ◐ (worker findings on Anthropic's published prompts; corroborated in [1]). Gemini Deep Research enforces budgets at the SKU level (~80 vs ~160 searches; ~250K vs ~900K input tokens) [fact-check: ✅] ✓ [4][5]. GPT Researcher publishes per-run cost telemetry (~$0.40 / 5 minutes on o3-mini high) — rare for an open stack ◐ (worker findings). Anthropic's claim that "token usage by itself explains 80% of BrowseComp performance variance" ◐ [1] is contested by PwC's "Cited but Not Verified" controlled ablation, where Fact-Check accuracy *degrades* by 42% on average as tool calls scale 2→150 (GPT-5.4: 79%→17%) while Link-Works stays above 92% — direct evidence that "more search" can reduce faithfulness ◐ (worker findings).

## Prompt Injection and the Lethal Trifecta

**Simon Willison's lethal trifecta** — private data + untrusted content + external communication — is the operative practitioner frame: combining all three in one session is the exploit, and "guardrails won't protect you" because detection-based filters can be bypassed adaptively ✓ [17]. **Meta's October 2025 Agents Rule of Two** [19] generalises exfiltration to "change state or communicate externally" and says an agent must satisfy at most two of {untrusted input, sensitive systems, state-change/external-comm} per session — otherwise human approval is required ✓ [18][19]. The November 2025 "Attacker Moves Second" paper bypassed 12 published prompt-injection / jailbreak defenses with >90% adaptive-attack success and 100% under human red-teaming ✓ [18]. Architectural separation is therefore the first-class defense.

Concretely, Anthropic's `web_fetch` URL-must-come-from-context invariant breaks the C-leg even when malicious page text instructs the model to POST secrets to `evil.example` — that URL is not yet in conversation, so the call refuses [fact-check: ✅] ✓ [20]. Meta's Email-Bot example recommends the same pattern: "limiting web requests to URLs exclusively returned from trusted sources" ✓ [19]. The Rule-of-Two corollary for a research pipeline: keep private data with the **planner** (which never directly ingests untrusted page text); restrict the **worker** to fetch tools that cannot construct attacker-URLs; deny the **writer/citation-auditor** any state-changing or external-communication tool — it should only emit prose and citations ? [17][19][20].

## Evaluation: What Each Benchmark Actually Measures

Short-answer benchmarks score persistence and tool use against a key. **BrowseComp** (1,266 multi-hop browsing questions; reference-matched) explicitly "sidesteps challenges of a true user query distribution, like generating long answers or resolving ambiguity" ◐ (worker findings). **GAIA** (466 questions, three tool-complexity levels; humans 92% vs GPT-4 plugins 15%) holds out 300/466 answers to resist contamination [fact-check: ✅] ✓ [27]. **HLE** (Phan et al., 2025) is 2,500–3,000 expert-written items across math/sciences/humanities — and is *not* a deep-research benchmark ✓ [28]. FutureHouse's audit found ~29% of HLE chemistry/biology answers literature-contradicted; the HLE team replicated at ~18% under stricter three-expert review [fact-check: ✅] ✓ [23][28].

Long-form benchmarks score synthesis. **USTC's DeepResearch Bench** (Du et al., 2025) is 100 PhD-level tasks (50 EN/50 ZH, 22 fields) with **RACE** (reference-based LLM-as-judge over comprehensiveness/depth/instruction-following/readability with adaptive criteria + dynamic weights — removing the reference drops human-correlation from 72.56 to 68.19) and **FACT** (statement–URL extraction + support verification yielding effective citations and citation accuracy) [fact-check: ✅] ✓ [24]. Reported leaderboard: Gemini 2.5 Pro DR 48.88 / 111 effective citations; OpenAI DR 46.98 / 41; Perplexity DR 42.25 with the highest citation accuracy 90.24% [fact-check: ✅] ✓ [24]. **FutureSearch's homonymous Deep Research Bench** is a *different* artifact: 91 tasks with 10–100k frozen webpages each via **RetroSearch** for reproducible re-runs, capped at 50 ReAct actions/task, leaderboard reports score + cost + runtime; ChatGPT o3 outperformed OpenAI Deep Research on the May 2025 snapshot ✓ [25]. Sources differ: [24] reports OpenAI DR > GPT-4.1+search on long-form synthesis while [25] reports o3 > OpenAI DR on extractive-answer tasks — the benchmarks measure different things, and practitioner posts conflate them; cite the arXiv ID, not just the name.

LLM-as-judge sits underneath all long-form benchmarks and carries documented position, verbosity, and self-preference bias; standard mitigations are dual-direction prompting with order swap, ensemble judges combining pairwise + rubric coverage, and reference-based scoring (the dominant signal in DRB's RACE) ◐ [24].

## Domain Verification Bars

**Legal**: Stanford RegLab/HAI's preregistered audit of Lexis+ AI, Westlaw AI-Assisted Research, and Ask Practical Law AI on 200+ queries reported hallucination rates of 17%, 33%, and 17% respectively versus 43% for GPT-4 — contradicting "hallucination-free" marketing claims and introducing a correctness-vs-groundedness taxonomy where "misgrounded" citations (real case, doesn't support the claim) are arguably worse than fabrication because they look authoritative [fact-check: ✅] ✓ [26]. They also document sycophancy on false-premise queries — making false-premise probes a required eval component ✓ [26].

**Medical**: OpenAI's HealthBench is 5,000 physician-written multi-turn conversations with 48,562 unique criteria from 262 physicians in 60 countries × 26 specialties, scored along accuracy / completeness / context awareness / communication / instruction-following — a rubric eval (per-conversation custom criteria), not exact-match, with a HealthBench Hard subset where top-model scores plummet [fact-check: ✅] ◐ [29]. The bar medicine adds is **uncertainty handling and context-seeking** — penalising confident-but-unsafe answers and rewarding "ask for missing info" ◐ [29].

## Open-Source Stacks and Reproducibility

The open stacks span from single-agent loops (HuggingFace's smolagents code-action `CodeAgent`, 55.15% on GAIA validation in 24 hours of work, vs OpenAI Deep Research's 67.36%) to full supervisor/worker hierarchies (LangChain's open_deep_research with three-phase Scope/Research/Write; GPT Researcher's optional STORM-inspired ChiefEditor→Editor→Researcher→Writer→Reviewer→Reviser pipeline) ◐ (worker findings). The gap to closed stacks is sharpest on **reproducibility primitives**: none of the four open stacks ships a frozen-corpus replay layer like RetroSearch by default — they all hit the live web through Tavily/Serper/DuckDuckGo, so reruns are not bit-reproducible. Cost telemetry is uneven; deterministic seeds are essentially impossible to enforce because LLM providers don't guarantee them and live-search results drift — which is precisely RetroSearch's motivation ◐ (worker findings on FutureSearch's DRB and RetroSearch design).
