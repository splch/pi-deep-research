# Ion-trap vs superconducting qubits for NISQ-era chemistry: trapped ions still hold the fidelity lead, superconductors hold the scale lead, and neither has cracked chemical accuracy at non-trivial size

**Date**: 2026-05-05
**Audience**: Technically literate researcher / engineer (graduate-level QC literacy) deciding which platform to use, build on, or read more about for chemistry-flavored NISQ work.
**Scope**: Variational ground-state energy estimation (VQE family) and related near-term molecular-simulation algorithms on transmon and trapped-ion processors, ~2017–early-2026.

## TL;DR

- **Trapped ions win on per-gate quality and connectivity**: world-record two-qubit gate fidelities reached 99.99% in October 2025 (IonQ/Oxford Ionics) and 99.914% across all pairs in production at Quantinuum H1-1 (April 2024); both platforms deliver native all-to-all connectivity ([IonQ news](https://www.ionq.com/news/ionq-achieves-landmark-result-setting-new-world-record-in-quantum-computing); [Quantinuum blog](https://www.quantinuum.com/blog/quantinuum-extends-its-significant-lead-in-quantum-computing-achieving-historic-milestones-for-hardware-fidelity-and-quantum-volume)).
- **Superconducting wins on speed, scale, and total gate-count budget**: IBM Heron r2 has 156 fixed-frequency transmons in a heavy-hex lattice and runs certain mirrored-Ising circuits with ≈5,000 two-qubit gates accurately, ~50× faster wall-clock than 2023 ([IBM newsroom 2024](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2024-11-13-ibm-launches-its-most-advanced-quantum-computers,-fueling-new-scientific-value-and-progress-towards-quantum-advantage)).
- **Largest published chemistry runs reflect the split**: 12 superconducting qubits for H₆/H₈/H₁₀/H₁₂ + diazene on Sycamore in 2020 ([Arute et al., Science](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04174)); 77 superconducting qubits on Heron for the [4Fe-4S] iron-sulfur cluster via SQD in 2024 ([Robledo-Moreno et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.05068v1)); H₂O to chemical accuracy on a trapped-ion device with no error mitigation in 2020 ([Nam et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41534-020-0259-3)); error-corrected QPE for H₂ at 22 ion-trap qubits in 2025 ([Quantinuum/QuantumInsider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2025/05/22/quantum-chemistry-gets-error-corrected-boost-from-quantinuums-trapped-ion-computer/)).
- **Neither platform has solved chemistry**: IonQ's own VQE benchmark on H₂…H₁₈ chains has a 1 mHa "solved" criterion that "currently remains unmet across the quantum computing industry" ([IonQ benchmarking blog](https://www.ionq.com/blog/beyond-qubit-counts-introducing-ionqs-application-centric-benchmarking-framework)).
- **The right platform depends on what you optimize**: deeper-circuit / larger-active-space chemistry (SQD, LUCJ, hydrogen chains) currently runs on superconducting hardware; shallow-ansatz / connectivity-bound chemistry (uPCCD, ADAPT-VQE on small molecules, error-corrected H₂) currently runs on ion traps.

## Background

Quantum chemistry — specifically the electronic-structure problem of finding ground-state energies of molecules — is the most-studied near-term application of quantum computers. The dominant NISQ-era algorithms are variants of the Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE), which uses a parameterized quantum circuit (the "ansatz") to prepare a trial wavefunction whose energy is estimated by repeated measurement, and a classical optimizer to update the parameters. Common ansätze include unitary coupled cluster with singles and doubles (UCCSD), hardware-efficient ansätze, ADAPT-VQE (which grows the circuit operator-by-operator), and pair-correlated variants such as unitary pair coupled-cluster doubles (uPCCD).

Two solid-state-physics platforms dominate practical NISQ chemistry today: superconducting transmon qubits (IBM, Google, Rigetti) and trapped-ion qubits (IonQ, Quantinuum, AQT). The two have very different physical strengths: ions live in vacuum chambers and are manipulated by lasers (Quantinuum, IonQ Forte) or microwaves (Oxford Ionics' "electronic qubit control"); transmons are lithographically defined LC circuits run at ~10 mK. The former are slow but extraordinarily clean; the latter are fast but noisier and more constrained in connectivity. Below, I unpack what those statements actually mean for chemistry.

## Hardware metrics circa 2024–2025

The cleanest single-number summary is the median two-qubit gate fidelity, because this number tends to dominate the achievable circuit depth.

For trapped ions, Quantinuum's H1-1 reached 99.914(3)% across all qubit pairs in production in April 2024, the first "three-nines" claim in a continuously-available commercial system ([Quantinuum 2024](https://www.quantinuum.com/blog/quantinuum-extends-its-significant-lead-in-quantum-computing-achieving-historic-milestones-for-hardware-fidelity-and-quantum-volume)). The H2-1 device (56 qubits, racetrack QCCD geometry) followed at 99.843(5)% with arbitrary connectivity in June 2024 ([Quantinuum H2-1 press release](https://www.quantinuum.com/press-releases/quantinuum-launches-industry-first-trapped-ion-56-qubit-quantum-computer-that-challenges-the-worlds-best-supercomputers)). IonQ's Forte was independently benchmarked at 30 qubits with all-to-all operations across all 435 pairs and passed Algorithmic Qubit benchmarks up to AQ 29 ([Chen et al., Quantum 2024](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2024-11-07-1516/)); IonQ's investor materials list a 99.6% native two-qubit gate fidelity on Forte at 600 µs gate time as of mid-2024 ([IonQ Q2 2024 deck](https://s28.q4cdn.com/828571518/files/doc_financials/2024/q2/24-08-07-Investor-Updates-Q2-2024_vF.pdf)). In October 2025 IonQ — having absorbed Oxford Ionics — reported the first 99.99% ("four nines") two-qubit gate, beating the previous Oxford Ionics 99.97% record ([IonQ news](https://www.ionq.com/news/ionq-achieves-landmark-result-setting-new-world-record-in-quantum-computing)).

For superconducting transmons, IBM's Heron family (133 qubits in r1, 156 in r2, both with tunable couplers in a heavy-hexagonal lattice) is currently the production target ([IBM Quantum docs](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/en/guides/processor-types)). Median two-qubit gate error on a Heron-class device improved from ≈5×10⁻³ to ≈3×10⁻³ over 2024 (i.e., from ~99.5% to ~99.7% fidelity), and Heron yields a "3-5x improvement in device performance over Eagle" ([IBM 2024](https://www.ibm.com/quantum/blog/quantum-roadmap-2033); [PostQuantum coverage](https://postquantum.com/industry-news/ibm-heron-r2-quantum/)). Coherence times have been climbing: IBM's ibm_kyiv reported median T₁=288 µs and T₂=127 µs at the device-array level ([Castro et al., EPJ QT](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1140/epjqt/s40507-025-00405-7)). At the single-qubit research-grade level, Fermilab/SQMS reported transmons with average T₁≈300 µs and max ≈600 µs in 2024 ([Bal et al., npj QI](https://news.fnal.gov/2024/05/researchers-at-the-sqms-center-achieve-leading-performance-in-transmon-qubits/)), and Aalto/VTT broke 1 ms T₂-echo for the first time in 2025 ([Tuokkola et al., Nat. Commun.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-61126-0)). These last numbers are not yet in deployed multi-qubit chips.

The qualitative tradeoff is summarized cleanly in independent compilations such as [QIR/MIT's benchmarking aggregation](https://qir.mit.edu/benchmarking/): "Trapped Ions have shown consistent growth and demonstrated the highest overall fidelity. … the Trapped Ion devices from Quantinuum and Oxford Ionics … reached a 0.999 ('triple-nine') fidelity, an important rubicon. However, this was achieved with relatively smaller qubit sizes. Amongst Superconducting QPUs, Google and IBM are class leaders, with the IBM's Heron r2 achieving the highest performance across this benchmark."

## Largest molecules simulated on each platform

The published, end-to-end chemistry experiments break out roughly as follows.

On superconducting hardware:

- **2017 — IBM (Kandala et al., Nature)**: hardware-efficient VQE on a 6-qubit transmon device ran H₂, LiH, and BeH₂ ground-state estimation with Hamiltonians of >100 Pauli terms ([arXiv 1704.05018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.05018); [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature23879)). This was the first VQE on hardware to go beyond H/He.
- **2020 — Google AI Quantum (Arute, Babbush et al., Science)**: VQE Hartree–Fock on Sycamore for hydrogen chains H₆ through H₁₂ and the diazene (N₂H₂) cis–trans isomerization, on up to 12 superconducting qubits — at the time, twice the qubit count and >10× the gate count of any prior chemistry experiment ([arXiv 2004.04174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04174); [Google research blog](https://research.google/blog/scaling-up-fundamental-quantum-chemistry-simulations-on-quantum-hardware/)).
- **2024 — IBM/RIKEN/Fugaku (Robledo-Moreno et al.)**: sample-based quantum diagonalization (SQD) on a 133-qubit Heron with subsets of 45–77 qubits to model the [2Fe-2S] and [4Fe-4S] iron-sulfur clusters and N₂ in cc-pVDZ. The largest run used **77 qubits**, ≈3,500 two-qubit gates per LUCJ circuit, and 3 million shots per data point ([arXiv 2405.05068](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.05068v1); [CERN talk](https://indico.cern.ch/event/1338689/contributions/6080333/attachments/2952086/5189637/2024-10-22-CHEP_IBM_VoicaRadescu.pdf)).

On trapped-ion hardware:

- **2018 — Innsbruck/Hempel et al.**: 4 qubits of a 20-qubit ion trap for VQE on H₂ and LiH ([AQT writeup](https://www.aqt.eu/news_quantum_chemistry/)).
- **2020 — IonQ (Nam et al., npj QI)**: VQE for H₂O ground state on a Yb⁺ ion-trap, reaching errors approaching the chemical-accuracy bound (1.6 mHa) without any error mitigation ([Nam et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41534-020-0259-3); [Duke iontrap](https://iontrap.duke.edu/files/2025/03/s41534-020-0259-3.pdf)).
- **2023 — IonQ Aria + Harmony (Liu et al., npj QI)**: orbital-optimized uPCCD VQE for LiH dissociation on Aria and Harmony, exploiting all-to-all connectivity to keep the LiH circuit at 3 qubits / 4 CX gates ([Liu et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41534-023-00730-8)).
- **2024 — Microsoft + Quantinuum (Azure Quantum blog)**: 12 logical qubits created on Quantinuum H1 via Microsoft's qubit-virtualization scheme, with **2 logical qubits used** for the active space of a catalytic intermediate. Logical-qubit results were more accurate than the same calculation on the underlying physical qubits ([Microsoft Azure blog 2024](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/blog/quantum/2024/09/10/microsoft-and-quantinuum-create-12-logical-qubits-and-demonstrate-a-hybrid-end-to-end-chemistry-simulation/)).
- **2025 — Quantinuum**: error-corrected quantum phase estimation for the H₂ ground state on H1, using a seven-qubit color code per logical qubit, **22 physical qubits**, >2,000 two-qubit gates and hundreds of mid-circuit measurements; energy converged to within 0.018 Ha of exact (above 0.0016 Ha chemical accuracy but the first end-to-end fault-tolerant chemistry run on real hardware) ([QuantumInsider summary](https://thequantuminsider.com/2025/05/22/quantum-chemistry-gets-error-corrected-boost-from-quantinuums-trapped-ion-computer/)).

The pattern is striking: superconducting hardware now hosts the largest physical-qubit chemistry runs (77 qubits, [4Fe-4S]) thanks to scale and SQD's tolerance for noisy samples; trapped-ion hardware hosts the smallest *but most reliable* runs, including the only error-corrected chemistry to date.

## Circuit depth budget and ansatz overhead

Connectivity is the lever that converts hardware fidelity into algorithmic reach. Trapped ions provide native all-to-all entanglement via shared phonon modes; transmons in heavy-hex or square lattices entangle only with nearest neighbors (Heron qubits connect to at most three others — see [IBM docs](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/en/guides/processor-types)).

The original 5-qubit head-to-head benchmark of [Linke et al. (PNAS 2017)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5380037/) made the consequence concrete: "reducing a fully connected system to the more sparse star-shaped or linear nearest-neighbor connectivity requires an increase in the number of gates of [O(N²)]," and "the fidelity of the fully connected ion-trap implementation is [higher than] the superconducting device" on every algorithm tested, with the gap growing as connectivity demand grew. For chemistry, this means UCCSD on a heavy-hex transmon array pays a SWAP tax that grows roughly quadratically with active-space size — exactly when the simulation starts to be interesting.

Trapped-ion implementations exploit this structurally. IonQ's uPCCD work for LiH compresses the molecule into 3 qubits and 4 CX gates by pair-mapping electrons and freezing irrelevant orbitals ([Liu et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41534-023-00730-8)). The IonQ blog is explicit about why: "we did not limit entanglement to the 'nearest neighbor' of any given qubit. These long-range entanglements can be done very efficiently on trapped-ion systems. This contrasts with superconducting systems, which would need to use more gates to accomplish the same effect" ([IonQ blog](https://www.ionq.com/blog/orbital-optimized-pair-correlated-electron-simulations-on-trapped-ion)).

The flip side is that absolute circuit depth on superconducting hardware has scaled aggressively. IBM's 2024 announcement claims accurate execution of "certain classes of quantum circuits with up to 5,000 two-qubit gate operations" on Heron ([IBM newsroom](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2024-11-13-ibm-launches-its-most-advanced-quantum-computers,-fueling-new-scientific-value-and-progress-towards-quantum-advantage)). That is a Mirrored-Ising-style benchmark, not a chemistry circuit, but it sets a credible upper bound: a chemistry workload with thousands of CX gates is plausible on Heron *if* it can be written in a heavy-hex-friendly form. The SQD [4Fe-4S] LUCJ circuit at ~3.5k two-qubit gates ([Robledo-Moreno et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.05068v1)) is exactly such a workload. Quantinuum H2 emulators carry a 1.05×10⁻³ two-qubit gate error and routinely run chemistry circuits in the hundreds of CX-equivalents ([Quantinuum blog](https://www.quantinuum.com/blog/automated-quantum-algorithm-discovery-for-quantum-chemistry)).

## Wall-clock and shot cost

Gate-time differences are about four orders of magnitude. Superconducting two-qubit gates take ~10–50 ns; trapped-ion gates take ~100 µs to 1 ms — IonQ Forte's two-qubit gate is 600 µs, with an aspirational target of 300 µs in 2025 ([IonQ Q2 2024](https://s28.q4cdn.com/828571518/files/doc_financials/2024/q2/24-08-07-Investor-Updates-Q2-2024_vF.pdf); [comparison table](https://entangledfuture.com/compare/superconducting-vs-trapped-ion/)). Worse, in segmented QCCD architectures most wall-clock time is *not* spent gating: IonQ's own materials disclose that "on average only 1-2% of wall clock time spent computing" — the rest is shuttling, cooling, and reconfiguration ([IonQ Q2 2024](https://s28.q4cdn.com/828571518/files/doc_financials/2024/q2/24-08-07-Investor-Updates-Q2-2024_vF.pdf)). The same point appears in independent architectural-simulation studies of segmented (QCCD) ion-trap designs, which find that the split / move / join shuttle primitives dominate runtime.

For VQE this matters because the algorithm is shot-hungry: each parameter update typically requires 10⁴–10⁶ shots over many Pauli terms, and the optimizer can take dozens to hundreds of iterations. IonQ themselves admit, in their application-centric benchmarking framework: "On variational algorithms of this class, architectures with faster gate speeds hold a TTS [time-to-solution] advantage, a tradeoff this framework reports directly rather than omits" ([IonQ benchmarking blog](https://www.ionq.com/blog/beyond-qubit-counts-introducing-ionqs-application-centric-benchmarking-framework)). The largest IBM SQD chemistry experiment ran circuits up to 3 million times each with ~45-minute QPU runtime per experiment ([Robledo-Moreno et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.05068v1)) — a workload that would be prohibitive on present-day ion-trap throughput.

## Error mitigation

Both platforms rely heavily on error mitigation in the absence of full quantum error correction. The toolkits overlap but are weighted differently:

- **Zero-noise extrapolation (ZNE)** was the original IBM/Kandala 2017 trick for BeH₂ (then under the name "Richardson extrapolation") ([IBM blog](https://www.ibm.com/quantum/blog/quantum-molecule)). It remains the dominant mitigation on superconducting chemistry workloads, often combined with randomized compiling for up to 100× error reduction on small VQE molecules ([Tsubouchi et al., Quantum 2023](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2023-11-20-1184/)).
- **Probabilistic error cancellation (PEC)** is more aggressive but more expensive in shots; van den Berg et al. demonstrated PEC with sparse Pauli–Lindblad models on IBM Eagle ([cited in Tsubouchi et al.](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2023-11-20-1184/)).
- **Symmetry verification / post-selection** is used widely on both platforms (N-representability post-selection in the Sycamore HF paper; Partition Measurement Symmetry Verification on Quantinuum H2 chemistry runs at LiH and BeH₂ ([Quantinuum blog](https://www.quantinuum.com/blog/automated-quantum-algorithm-discovery-for-quantum-chemistry))).
- **Purification-based / virtual-distillation** mitigation has been demonstrated to reduce errors up to 100× on chemistry circuits ([Nature Physics 2022 summary](https://communities.springernature.com/posts/pushing-the-boundaries-of-nisq-with-error-mitigation)).

The Caltech / Preskill perspective is the right cautionary frame: "[ZNE/PEC] work effectively at extending the size of the circuits we can execute with useful fidelity. But the asymptotic cost scales exponentially with the size of the circuit, so error mitigation alone may not suffice to reach quantum value" ([Quantum Frontiers](https://quantumfrontiers.com/2023/12/09/crossing-the-quantum-chasm-from-nisq-to-fault-tolerance/)). For chemistry specifically, this is why the Quantinuum / Microsoft logical-qubit experiments are notable: they substitute hardware quality for shot overhead.

## Practical implications

If your NISQ chemistry workload is *small but tightly correlated* — multireference active spaces of 4–10 spatial orbitals, or any case where ansatz quality matters more than width — trapped ions are the better fit today. The combination of ~99.9% gate fidelity, all-to-all connectivity (no SWAP tax for UCCSD/uPCCD/ADAPT-VQE), and seconds-scale coherence means you can run deeper or more expressive ansätze and get cleaner energy estimates per shot. The Quantinuum/Microsoft logical-qubit pipeline is currently the only path to error-corrected molecular energies on hardware ([Microsoft Azure 2024](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/blog/quantum/2024/09/10/microsoft-and-quantinuum-create-12-logical-qubits-and-demonstrate-a-hybrid-end-to-end-chemistry-simulation/); [QuantumInsider 2025](https://thequantuminsider.com/2025/05/22/quantum-chemistry-gets-error-corrected-boost-from-quantinuums-trapped-ion-computer/)).

If your workload is *wide and shallow*, dominated by Hamiltonian sampling, or amenable to recent classical-coupled methods like SQD/LUCJ — large active spaces, hydrogen chains, transition-metal clusters where you need many qubits but tolerate moderate per-shot error because thousands of shots are cheap — superconducting hardware is currently the only platform that can fit the qubits, run the millions of shots in reasonable wall-clock, and integrate with HPC. The IBM/RIKEN/Fugaku [4Fe-4S] result is the proof point ([Robledo-Moreno et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.05068v1); [QC Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/riken-and-ibm-demonstrate-quantum-centric-supercomputing-at-scale/)).

Both platforms are *still* short of chemical accuracy on any non-trivial molecule when run end-to-end on hardware. The IonQ benchmark is blunt about it: "This standard currently remains unmet across the quantum computing industry" ([IonQ blog](https://www.ionq.com/blog/beyond-qubit-counts-introducing-ionqs-application-centric-benchmarking-framework)). What has changed in 2024–2025 is the appearance of credible *paths* to chemical accuracy: SQD + HPC for superconducting; logical qubits + early fault-tolerance for trapped ion. Which path delivers first is genuinely open.

## What I couldn't verify

- **Direct apples-to-apples chemistry benchmark across platforms in 2024–2025**: I could not find a single peer-reviewed paper that runs the same molecule with the same ansatz on a current-generation Quantinuum/IonQ device *and* an IBM Heron and reports comparable energy errors. The 2017 Linke et al. comparison ([PNAS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5380037/)) is now eight years old. The recent [arXiv 2603.27397](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27397v2) preprint compares AQT IBEX Q1 to IBM Fez/Brisbane, but on protocol-level benchmarks rather than chemistry. Treat the cross-platform claims here as inferences from per-platform reports.
- **Median 2-qubit fidelities for ibm_fez (Heron r2) vs ibm_kyiv (Eagle r3)**: IBM does not publish device-by-device median 2Q fidelity in a single canonical place; the ~99.7% Heron-class number in this report combines IBM's own qualitative "3-5× over Eagle" claim with secondary reporting ([PostQuantum 2024](https://postquantum.com/industry-news/ibm-heron-r2-quantum/)). Per-device daily calibration data is published in the Qiskit backend properties but I did not pull and aggregate it.
- **The 99.99% IonQ four-nines result**: as of October 2025 it appeared in an IonQ press release and an Oxford Ionics blog post that read like the same text ([IonQ news](https://www.ionq.com/news/ionq-achieves-landmark-result-setting-new-world-record-in-quantum-computing); [Oxford Ionics blog](https://www.oxionics.com/blogs/accelerating-towards-fault-tolerance-unlocking-99-99-two-qubit-gate-fidelities/)). I did not verify the technical paper itself; the cited numbers should be treated as vendor-reported until a peer-reviewed write-up is available.
- **Specific shot counts and wall-clock for trapped-ion VQE chemistry**: most published trapped-ion chemistry papers (e.g. [Liu et al. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41534-023-00730-8); [Nam et al. 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41534-020-0259-3)) report finite-shot error bars but not total clock time of the VQE optimization. I extrapolated the throughput discussion from gate-time and shuttling overhead numbers rather than measured end-to-end clock times.
- **"Largest molecule" is loosely defined**: I treat qubit count and 2Q-gate count of the executed circuit as the primary axis, but a 10-qubit run on H₂ in a large basis is not the same scientific achievement as a 10-qubit run on a transition-metal cluster. The [4Fe-4S] / 77-qubit / ~3.5k-CX SQD result is, by any reasonable measure, the most ambitious chemistry workload on any quantum hardware to date.
