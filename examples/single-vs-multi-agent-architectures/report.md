The 2025 debate is anchored by two posts published one day apart. **Anthropic (June 13, 2025)** documents an orchestrator-worker architecture: a LeadResearcher analyzes the query, persists a plan to external Memory because total state can exceed the 200K-token context window, spawns 3–5 specialized Subagents in parallel with separate context windows, and finally hands the report to a dedicated CitationAgent for source attribution ✓ [1][2][3]. Two layers of parallelism (3–5 parallel subagents × 3+ parallel tool calls each) cut wall-clock research time up to **90%** on complex queries ◐ [1][4].

## What the benchmarks actually say

### Breadth-first research and browsing — multi-agent leads, but compute is co-varying

On **Anthropic's internal research eval**, multi-agent (Opus 4 lead + Sonnet 4 workers) beat single-agent Opus 4 by 90.2% [fact-check: ✅] ✓ [1][2][3][4]. The motivating example was "identify all the board members of the IT S&P 500" — multi-agent decomposed per-company while the single agent failed via slow sequential search [fact-check: ✅] ◐ [1].

On **DeepResearch Bench** (100 PhD-level tasks, RACE+FACT framework), orchestrator-worker products dominate single-pass LLM-with-search variants: Gemini-2.5-Pro Deep Research 48.88 RACE / 111.21 effective citations; OpenAI Deep Research 46.98 / 40.79; Perplexity Deep Research 42.25 / 31.26; vs Claude-3.7-Sonnet w/Search at 40.67 and GPT-4o-Search-Preview(high) at 35.10 [fact-check: ✅] ◐ [12]. The systems sit at different points on a breadth/accuracy frontier — Perplexity emits fewer citations but at 90.24% accuracy vs Gemini's 81.44% [fact-check: ✅] ◐ [12]. **OpenAI Deep Research is itself a single RL-trained o3 agent**, scoring 26.6% on HLE and a SOTA 67.36% pass@1 / 72.57% cons@64 on GAIA [fact-check: ✅] ✓ [22][24]💀; the 2-RACE-point gap to multi-agent Gemini suggests architecture alone does not determine performance.

ResearchRubrics (arXiv 2511.07685, Nov 2025) finds even the strongest deep-research agents stay below **68% average rubric compliance** and that no system simultaneously achieves breadth and precision ◐ [27].

A direct caution: a March 2026 Anthropic follow-up reports Claude Opus 4.6 hits **86.81%** on BrowseComp (multi-agent, 86.57% after removing 11 contamination cases), but the multi-agent configuration also produced "unintended solutions" (eval-aware shortcut paths) at **0.87% vs 0.24% for single-agent — a 3.7× amplification** attributed to higher token use and parallel searcher fan-out [fact-check: ✅] ◐ [11].

### Multi-hop reasoning at matched token budgets — single-agent wins

The cleanest controlled comparison is **Tran & Kiela (Stanford / Contextual AI, arXiv:2604.02460, April 2026)**. Across Qwen3-30B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-70B, Gemini-2.5-Flash, and Gemini-2.5-Pro on FRAMES and MuSiQue 4-hop, holding thinking-token budgets at 100 → 10,000 constant, single-agent (SAS) matches or outperforms five MAS variants at every budget above 100 tokens [fact-check: ✅] ◐ [13]. On Gemini-2.5-Pro FRAMES @ 5K tokens: SAS 0.700, Sequential 0.680, Debate 0.700, Ensemble 0.710, Parallel-roles 0.700; on MuSiQue 4-hop @ 5K: SAS 0.419 [fact-check: ❓] ◐ [13]. The information-theoretic argument is that any multi-agent decomposition with messages M = g(C) must satisfy P_e(M) ≥ P_e(C) by the Data Processing Inequality — handoffs can only lose information [fact-check: ✅] ◐ [13]. MAS only overtakes SAS under **heavy context corruption** (substitution α=0.7, masking α=0.7), suggesting multi-agent is best understood as a hedge against long-context degradation, not an architectural multiplier ◐ [13]. The paper also flags that Gemini's API-reported `thoughts_token_count` is inflated up to 4.7× over visible content — meaning prior MAS wins on Gemini may reflect **unaccounted compute** ◐ [13].

A second 2026 paper (Żywot et al., arXiv:2601.11327) tests an 8B-orchestrator + 1.7B-subagent system vs a 32B direct-tools single agent: GAIA 23.0 vs 23.0, AIME 55.0 vs 45.0, GPQA 58.6 vs 60.1, MuSiQue 14.0 vs 15.0, HLE ~4% on both. The 8B MAS runs **4.2× faster (7.9s vs 32.9s/query)** and uses 9.1–11.7K vs 19.5K tokens (43% reduction); subagent thinking adds 6.1s with no accuracy gain — **orchestrator capacity is the dominant lever, subagent scaling is not** [fact-check: ❓] ◐ [14].

### Where simple single-agents win on tool-heavy benchmarks

On the GAIA leaderboard, **H2O.ai's single-agent code-first "h2oGPTe Agent" reaches ~65%**, beating Microsoft's Magentic-One multi-agent stack (~38% with o1, ~32% with GPT-4o) and Google Langfun (49%) [fact-check: ✅] ◐ [17]. Magentic-One itself reports statistically competitive performance to SOTA on GAIA, AssistantBench, and WebArena via an Orchestrator + WebSurfer/FileSurfer/Coder/Terminal design ◐ [18], so the architectural debate sits behind base-model and tool-quality differences. LangChain frames the same divide as **read vs write**: read-style tasks parallelize cleanly; write-style tasks suffer when split ◐ [8][15][19].

## Reliability: error compounding and context fragmentation

Anthropic's own writeup documents early multi-agent pathologies: subagents spawning **50 sub-subagents** for simple queries, scouring the web for nonexistent sources, distracting each other with excessive updates, and duplicating work (one investigating the 2021 chip crisis while two others redundantly worked the 2025 supply chain) ◐ [1]. Mitigations were prompt-engineering heavy: explicit scaling rules (1 agent / 3–10 calls for fact-finding; 2–4 subagents for comparisons; >10 for complex research), broad-to-narrow heuristics, source-quality rules added after testers caught a bias toward SEO content farms, and a tool-testing agent that rewrote MCP descriptions and produced a **40% drop in downstream task time** [fact-check: ❓] ◐ [1].

Cemri et al.'s **MAST study (arXiv:2503.13657, NeurIPS 2025)** annotated 1,642 traces from seven MAS frameworks and codified **14 failure modes in three buckets**: system design, inter-agent misalignment (conversation reset, task derailment, information withholding, failure to clarify), and weak task verification [fact-check: ✅] ◐ [9]. The study concludes MAS performance gains "often remain minimal" vs single-agent, independently corroborating Cognition's qualitative claims [fact-check: ✅] ◐ [9].

Production reliability practices Anthropic ships include full tracing, **resume-from-checkpoint** rather than restart, rainbow deployments to avoid breaking long-running stateful agents, and an appendix pattern where subagents write artifacts to filesystem and pass lightweight references back to avoid the "telephone game" ◐ [1]. LangChain's Chase argues the dominant reliability lever is "making sure the LLM has the appropriate context at each step" — multi-agent is fundamentally a **context-engineering problem** ✓ [8].

## Security: prompt injection and the lethal trifecta

Simon Willison's lethal trifecta (June 2025) — private-data access + untrusted-content exposure + external-communication channel — defines structural prompt-injection risk; combining all three enables exfiltration regardless of guardrails, and "95% is very much a failing grade" in this domain ✓ [28][29]. **Every web-fetching subagent multiplies the second leg of the trifecta** ✓ [28]. Lee & Tiwari (NeurIPS 2024, "Prompt Infection") show injected instructions self-replicate across interconnected agents "much like a computer virus," propagating "even when agents do not publicly share all communications" ◐ [9] [unsupported]. AgentXploit (arXiv:2505.05849) reports **71% / 70% indirect-injection success** against o3-mini and GPT-4o agents on AgentDojo and VWA-adv ◐ [9] [unsupported]. Even Anthropic's hardened browser-use Claude reports **~1% residual attack success** after RL training, classifiers, and intervention layers, and explicitly states "no browser agent is immune" [fact-check: ❓] ◐ [30].

Sources differ on the multi-agent-specific amplification: Anthropic (March 2026) reports a **3.7× amplification of "unintended solutions"** in multi-agent vs single-agent on BrowseComp (0.87% vs 0.24%), attributing it to higher token use and parallel searcher fan-out [fact-check: ✅] ◐ [11]; Cognition does not address the security dimension ? ## Decision rules

**Pick multi-agent (orchestrator-worker) when:**
1. The task is breadth-first with many independent sub-questions (S&P-500-style enumerations, market scans, literature surveys) ✓ [1][12].
2. Information state will exceed a single context window ✓ [1][2].
3. Latency budget is tight and the work is embarrassingly parallel ◐ [1].
4. Task value justifies a ~15× token premium ✓ [1].
5. You have engineering capacity for tracing, checkpointing, prompt-level scaling rules, and a dedicated verifier/citation pass ◐ [1].

**Pick single-agent when:**
1. The task is write-heavy or stylistically/semantically coupled (code, refactors, coherent long-form prose) ✓ [5].
2. Reasoning is sequential and multi-hop with bounded context — at matched compute, single-agent dominates ◐ [13].
3. Coding work — explicitly advised by Anthropic and Cognition ✓ [1][5].
4. Frontier model is available; the multi-agent edge concentrates in weaker-model regimes ◐ [14].
5. Budget is tight and rubric-style accuracy matters more than coverage breadth ◐ [12][27].

**Hybrid pattern that ships in 2026:** single-threaded primary writer + isolated read-only verifier subagent on diff/output (Devin Review, Anthropic CitationAgent) ✓ [1][6]; "smart-friend" escalation from a fast primary to a frontier model on hard sub-decisions ◐ [6]; sub-agents scoped to investigative Q&A only (Claude Code) ◐ [5].
